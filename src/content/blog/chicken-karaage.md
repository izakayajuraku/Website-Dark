Few dishes pair better with an ice-cold beer or a smooth glass of shochu than chicken karaage — Japan's answer to fried chicken. This beloved izakaya staple is known for its crispy, golden crust and juicy, flavorful interior. At Izakaya Juraku, we serve up versions that honor tradition while delivering maximum crunch and umami.

## The History of Karaage

Karaage (pronounced "kah-rah-ah-geh") dates back to the Edo period (1603–1868), when Japanese cooks began deep-frying foods influenced by Chinese and Portuguese techniques. Over time, the dish evolved into the signature Japanese-style fried chicken we know today — marinated in a blend of soy sauce, garlic, ginger, and other seasonings before being coated in potato starch and fried to perfection.

Unlike Western fried chicken, karaage is lighter and crispier, thanks to the starch coating, which creates an airy crunch while keeping the meat incredibly juicy.

## Chicken Karaage at Izakaya Juraku

At Izakaya Juraku, we take pride in serving chicken karaage that's packed with flavor and irresistibly crispy. We offer it in two delicious styles:

### Signature Karaage with Spicy Mayo

- **Marination:** a house blend of soy sauce, garlic, ginger, and a touch of sesame oil for depth
- **Coating:** a light potato starch crust that fries up perfectly crisp
- **Topping:** drizzled with our creamy, house-made spicy mayo for a rich and tangy kick
- **Serving style:** hot and golden brown, with a wedge of lemon for a bright citrus contrast
- **Pairing suggestion:** best enjoyed with a Kirishima Shochu Highball or a refreshing Japanese lager

### Oroshi Karaage with Yuzu Wasabi Dressing

- **Marination:** the same flavorful soy-based marinade as our signature karaage
- **Coating:** light and crispy, allowing the toppings to shine
- **Topping:** grated daikon radish with a refreshing yuzu wasabi dressing, adding a bright, zesty contrast
- **Serving style:** a lighter, more refreshing take on karaage, perfect for those who enjoy a balance of umami and acidity
- **Pairing suggestion:** pairs beautifully with a chilled glass of dry sake or a citrus-forward cocktail
